Observation weighting

A Pythagoras card

Nick Ward

🧭 Purpose

Inspect how observation weights distribute influence across the data. This card considers every variable assigned the Weighting role. It provides diagnostics, can reshape importance weights before target balancing, and saves a Test-weighting policy. Place it after role assignment and before Target Balance when adjusting weights.

🎯 Goals

  1. Primary: Identify concentrated or zero effective weights and their contributing components.
  2. Secondary: Adjust importance weights deliberately while preserving each component’s training mean.
  3. Tertiary: Specify whether Test evaluation uses importance weights or uniform weights.

🧠 How it works

An observation’s effective weight is the product of its assigned Weighting components. Names beginning with balance__ identify balancing factors; other Weighting variables are importance components.

Table 1 - Weight diagnostics
Term Meaning
Zero-weight fraction Proportion of observations whose effective weight is zero.
Effective sample size (∑w)2/∑w2(\sum w)^2/\sum w^2, a measure of weight concentration, not a count of independent observations. All-zero weights give zero.
Largest weight share Largest observation weight divided by total effective weight.

For a selected importance component, power pp gives

wi′=wip∑j∈Trainwj∑j∈Trainwjp.w'_i = w_i^p\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathrm{Train}}w_j}{\sum_{j\in\mathrm{Train}}w_j^p}.

Each component retains its training mean. All-zero components remain zero. Applying the same power to all components makes their product proportional to the original product raised to that power, but need not preserve its total. This is a nonnegative power transformation, not ordinary Box–Cox.

🏞️ Front

Normal view pairs blue effective-weight bars with an orange horizontal distribution chart. The bars show Observations against Weights; observation numbers are one-based input positions, not identifiers. Hover reports the observation and weight. The distribution shows Counts against Weights, with hover reporting interval bounds and counts. Both charts share the weight scale.

Full screen adds individual component charts when there are multiple Weighting variables. With one variable, both views show the same two charts. The bar display can be thinned to evenly spaced observations; the distribution and footer diagnostics always use all observations.

The footer reports zero weights, effective sample size, and largest weight share. More than 10% zero weights triggers a warning: downstream weighted consumers fall back to unweighted calculations. Exactly 10% is allowed. Missing, negative, nonnumeric, or infinite components produce an explanation instead of a plot. No assigned weightings or no observations also produces an explanatory message.

🔄 Flip side

There is no flip side. The front combines distributions and summary diagnostics; full screen provides the component detail.

🛠️ Controls and actions

The Weight shape buttons appear only when at least one importance weighting is assigned and no assigned weighting has a balance__ prefix. They are hidden for no weightings, balancing-only weightings, or mixed importance and balancing weightings.

Table 2 - Shape actions
Button Power Effect
Square 2 Increase relative extremes.
Original 1 Restore this card’s incoming weights.
Square root 0.5 Reduce relative extremes.
Cube root 1/3 Reduce relative extremes more strongly.

Actions change downstream data immediately; there is no Commit button. Component to transform selects one importance component or All importance components. Original reverses this card’s transformation while retaining its Test policy and pipeline entry.

When balancing weights are present, every incoming weight stays unchanged. Saved power selections are ignored while transformation is unavailable and may resume if the input becomes importance-only again. Power, component, and policy selections are bookmarkable.

🔎 Interpretation and next steps

There is no ideal distribution or requirement for normally distributed weights. Concentration, low effective sample size, and many zeros warrant investigation; they do not establish that weights are wrong. Reshaping importance weights changes the learning objective and may alter the meaning of sampling or other precisely defined corrections.

Transform importance weights first, then calculate target balancing weights. Changing either balancing factors or their accompanying importance weights can undo the intended class balance, even when component means are preserved. The restriction on shape buttons enforces this ordering. After balancing, use this card to inspect the combined weights.

⛔️ Limitations

  • Positive powers preserve zeros and cannot repair a large zero-weight fraction.
  • The charts are an all-data preview. Preview Test weighting illustrates a policy on current observations; it does not create held-out data.
  • Concentration diagnostics do not account for dependence between observations.
  • The evaluator must explicitly apply the saved weighting policy; saving it does not automatically make a model or scoring method consume weights.

📌 Suggested workflow

  1. Assign Weighting roles and check what each component represents.
  2. Inspect effective weights, warnings, and full-screen component charts.
  3. If justified, adjust importance weights before adding balancing factors.
  4. Apply target balancing downstream and inspect the resulting combined weights.
  5. Compare performance on untouched validation data using the intended Test policy.

🔗 Data flow

  • Input: Current data, Weighting roles, and the existing pipeline.
  • Output: A preview and an unfitted pipeline step. Normalization is learned within each training fold and reused on Test rows; selected importance columns must remain available. Rows, columns, roles, and the original clean frame are preserved. No weightings means unchanged pass-through with no added step.
  • Evaluation: Test weighting saves Importance only, excluding balance__ components, or Uniform (no weights). With balancing weights present, the step saves this policy without transforming components. Display settings do not change exported data.
  • Data journey: Assigned weightings add a Learning entry, “Configure observation weighting,” including when Original is selected or balancing weights prevent transformation. No assigned weightings add no entry.
